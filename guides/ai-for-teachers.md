# Using AI to Make Money: A Practical Guide for Middle School History Teachers

*A beginner-friendly playbook for turning your teaching materials and skills into side income, with AI doing most of the heavy lifting.*

> **A note on your niche.** Middle school social studies / history is a **strong** niche on TPT. Why: the curriculum is wide (ancient civilizations, US history, world history, geography, civics), classroom budgets are real, and elementary-trained teachers often feel under-resourced when they hit a 6th–8th grade social studies block. There's less competition than ELA or math, and DBQs, primary sources, simulations, and stations sell well. You're in a good spot.

## 1. Quick Orientation

### What this really looks like

AI doesn't print money. What it does is **collapse the time** between "I have an idea" and "I have a polished product, a sales page, marketing copy, and ten social posts." For a teacher, that's the difference between making one worksheet a month and shipping a full unit bundle in a weekend.

You will still do real work. But you'll do **a teacher's work**—curating, judging quality, knowing what students need—not the design, copywriting, and marketing grunt work.

### Three realistic paths

| Path | What you sell | Effort to start | Ceiling |
|------|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| **A. Teaching materials on a marketplace** | Worksheets, units, slides, assessments | Low | $500–$5,000/mo for most; top sellers earn six figures |
| **B. Teaching materials on your own site** | Same as A | Medium | Higher margin, slower start |
| **C. Tutoring, coaching, or courses** | Your time and expertise | Medium–High | Highest hourly rate |

**Recommendation:** start with **A**. It's the lowest-risk way to find out what people actually buy. Layer in B and C later.

### Honest expectations

- **Months 1–3:** You're learning the platform, building a small catalog, getting your first sales. Earnings: $0–$200/mo.
- **Months 4–12:** Catalog grows to 30–100 products. Earnings: $200–$2,000/mo if you're consistent.
- **Year 2+:** With email list, Pinterest traffic, and a few hit products, $2,000–$10,000/mo is realistic for serious sellers.

This is a **business**, not a lottery ticket. Treat it like one.

## 2. Pick Your Product

### Step 1: Inventory what you already have

Open a spreadsheet. List every teaching resource you've made in the last few years:

- Worksheets and practice sets
- Lesson plans and unit plans
- Slide decks
- Assessments, quizzes, exit tickets
- Anchor charts and visuals
- Project rubrics
- Sub plans
- Centers and stations
- Digital activities (Google Slides, Boom Cards, etc.)

Don't filter yet. Just list them.

### Step 2: Figure out what sells

Before polishing anything, do **15 minutes of market research**:

1. Go to **teacherspayteachers.com** (TPT).
2. Search the topics you teach. Try a mix of broad and narrow:
   - Broad: "middle school US history," "ancient civilizations unit," "7th grade world history"
   - Narrow: "Constitution stations," "Industrial Revolution DBQ," "Mesopotamia escape room," "Bill of Rights one pager"
3. Sort by "Best Sellers" and "Highest Rated."
4. Note: What formats sell? What's the price range? What do covers look like? How many pages?

**What sells well in middle school history specifically:**

- **DBQ-style document analysis** (primary sources with scaffolded questions)
- **One-pagers** (single-page creative summary projects — hugely popular)
- **Stations / gallery walks** (6–10 short activities students rotate through)
- **Escape rooms / breakouts** (digital or print puzzles around a topic)
- **Interactive notebook pages** (foldables, flipbooks)
- **Sketchnote / doodle notes** templates
- **Simulations and role-plays** (mock trials, "you are there" scenarios)
- **Editable Google Slides decks** with built-in activities
- **Sub plans** for history teachers (universally desperate market)
- **Test prep and review games** (Jeopardy decks, task cards)

**Topics with steady demand:**
- US: Constitution & Bill of Rights, Colonial America, Revolutionary War, Civil War, Reconstruction, Industrial Revolution, Civil Rights Movement
- World: Ancient Egypt, Greece, Rome, Mesopotamia, Medieval Europe, Renaissance, World Wars, Cold War
- Skills: primary vs. secondary sources, point of view, historical thinking, map skills, current events

Look for **gaps**—topics with weak listings or outdated designs. That's your opening.

### Step 3: Polish with AI

For each resource you want to sell, run it through this AI workflow. You can use **ChatGPT** (chatgpt.com) or **Claude** (claude.ai); both have free tiers that are plenty for this.

**Prompt 1 — Quality review:**
> "I'm a middle school history teacher selling teaching materials. Review this resource and suggest improvements. Look for: historical accuracy, clear instructions, grade-appropriate vocabulary for grades 6–8, missing answer keys, opportunities to differentiate, and bias or one-sidedness in source selection. Here is the resource: [paste]"

**Prompt 2 — Make variants:**
> "Create three differentiated versions of this history activity: (1) for struggling readers (Lexile ~700–800), (2) on grade level (Lexile ~900–1000), (3) extension for advanced students. Keep the same content focus and visual structure. Adjust vocabulary, sentence length, and scaffolding."

**Prompt 3 — Add an answer key:**
> "Generate a clean answer key for this history activity with brief teacher notes: (1) the expected response, (2) what a strong answer adds, (3) common student misconceptions, (4) optional follow-up question to push thinking."

**Prompt 4 — Write the product description:**
> "Write a TPT product description for this middle school history resource. Include: what's included (page count and formats), grade level (6–8), topic and unit fit, suggested uses (whole class, stations, sub day, homework), aligned C3 Framework or state social studies standards if relevant, and a brief teacher-to-teacher pitch. Keep it scannable with bullet points."

**Prompt 5 — Generate a primary source DBQ from scratch:**
> "Create a DBQ-style document analysis activity for 7th graders on [topic]. Include: 4 primary source excerpts (real, attributed, 100–200 words each), background context paragraph, sourcing questions for each document, and a culminating short-answer prompt. Cite each source. Flag any source where I should verify the wording against the original."

**⚠️ Critical for history:** AI gets historical facts wrong constantly — wrong dates, fabricated quotes, misattributed sources. **Always verify** primary source text against the original (Library of Congress, National Archives, Avalon Project at Yale, Internet History Sourcebooks). Use AI to draft and structure; use real sources for content.

### Step 4: Bundle

Single worksheets are commodity goods. **Bundles sell.** A 30-page unit at $8 beats six $1.50 worksheets every time. After you have 4–5 related products, package them as a bundle at a 25–30% discount.

## 3. Where to Sell

### Option A: Teachers Pay Teachers (start here)

- **URL:** teacherspayteachers.com
- **How it works:** You upload products, they handle payment and delivery, they take a cut.
- **Fees:**
  - **Free seller:** TPT keeps ~45% per sale. No upfront cost.
  - **Premium seller ($59.95/yr):** TPT keeps ~20% per sale. Pays for itself after ~$300 in sales.
- **Pros:** Built-in audience of millions of teachers. They search TPT directly. No need to drive traffic.
- **Cons:** Competitive. They own the customer relationship. They can change policies (and have).
- **Start as a free seller. Upgrade to Premium when you hit ~$300/mo.**

### Option B: Made By Teachers, Classful, Boom Learning

Secondary marketplaces. Worth listing on once your TPT catalog is established—it's just copy-paste uploads for extra exposure. Don't start here.

### Option C: Your own website (Etsy as a stepping stone)

- **Etsy** is a middle ground: more reach than your own site, less teacher-specific than TPT. Strong for **homeschool parents**, who are a real audience for middle school history (lots of homeschool families teach US history and ancient civilizations).
- **Your own site** (covered in Section 4) gives you the highest margin and a real business asset—but requires you to bring traffic.

**History-specific homeschool angle:** classical and Charlotte Mason homeschoolers buy heavily in your subject. Search Etsy for "ancient history homeschool" or "US history unit study" to see this market.

### Recommended path

```
Month 1–3:   TPT only
Month 4–6:   TPT + Etsy
Month 6+:    TPT + Etsy + your own site (and email list)
```

## 4. Setting Up Your Own Website

You do **not** need to learn web development. In 2026, you can have a real store online in an afternoon.

### Pick a platform

For a beginner teacher selling digital products, the choice is between three:

| Platform | Best for | Monthly cost | Setup difficulty |
|----------|----------|--------------|------------------|
| **Payhip** | Simple digital downloads | Free (5% fee) or $29/mo (0% fee) | ⭐ Easiest |
| **Gumroad** | Same | Free (10% fee) | ⭐ Easiest |
| **Shopify** | Real store with branding | $29/mo + fees | ⭐⭐ Medium |
| **Squarespace + Commerce** | Beautiful site, blog, store | $23–$40/mo | ⭐⭐ Medium |

**Recommendation:** Start with **Payhip**. Free, dead simple, handles digital delivery, EU VAT, and discount codes. You can graduate to Shopify later if you outgrow it.

### Pages you need

Keep it small. You need exactly five pages:

1. **Home** — who you are, what you sell, a "Shop now" button, an email signup
2. **Shop** — your products (Payhip generates this automatically)
3. **About** — your teaching story; this is the page that builds trust
4. **Free resource / email signup** — give away one good resource in exchange for an email
5. **Contact** — email form

That's it. No blog yet. No fancy navigation.

### Use AI to write every word

Don't agonize over copy. Use this prompt:

> "You're a copywriter for a teacher who sells [subject/grade] resources. Write the [home / about / shop intro] page in a warm, teacher-to-teacher voice. Avoid corporate buzzwords. Tone: experienced teacher sharing what works, not 'guru.' Here are the facts about me: [3–5 bullets about your background, what you teach, what makes your stuff different]."

Then edit. AI gets you to 80%; your voice gets you the last 20%.

### Domain and email

- **Domain:** Buy at Namecheap or Cloudflare (~$10/yr). Pick `[yourname]teaches.com` or `[subject]withmrs[lastname].com`.
- **Business email:** Google Workspace ($6/mo) for `you@yourname.com`. Looks more professional than `mrsteacher1987@gmail.com`.

### Payments and taxes (the boring but important part)

- Payhip/Gumroad/Shopify handle credit card processing for you via Stripe.
- **Taxes:** In the US, side income is reportable on Schedule C of your tax return. Track income and expenses in a simple spreadsheet from day one. If you cross ~$400 in profit, you owe self-employment tax.
- **Sales tax:** Payhip/Gumroad/Shopify handle this for you in most US states.
- **Talk to a tax preparer** once you're earning $1,000+ a month. It's worth the $300.

## 5. Marketing with AI

You can have the best worksheets in the world. If nobody sees them, they don't sell. This is the section most teachers skip and most teachers regret skipping.

### The single highest-leverage thing: an email list

- People who buy from a TPT search are strangers. People on your email list are **fans who asked to hear from you**.
- An email list is the only marketing channel you own. Pinterest, Instagram, and TPT can all change their algorithms tomorrow.

**How to start (one afternoon):**

1. Sign up for **MailerLite** (free up to 1,000 subscribers) or **ConvertKit/Kit** (free up to 10,000).
2. Create a **lead magnet**: one really good free resource (a week of warm-ups, a holiday activity pack, an editable rubric).
3. Add a signup form to your website's home page.
4. Send a welcome email when someone signs up. AI can write it.
5. Email your list **once a week or twice a month**. Mix free tips and product announcements.

**AI prompt for emails:**

> "Write a 200-word email to my list of [grade] teachers. The topic is [teaching tip / new product / seasonal idea]. Voice: friendly colleague, not a brand. Include one practical takeaway and a soft mention of [product name and link]. End with a question to encourage replies."

### Pinterest: the #1 channel for teacher-sellers

Teachers and homeschool parents search Pinterest like Google. A single viral pin can drive sales for years.

**The workflow:**

1. Set up a **business Pinterest account** in your store's name.
2. For each product, create **5–10 different pin designs** (vertical, 1000×1500px).
3. Use **Canva** (free) with its AI features to generate pin designs in bulk—Canva has teacher-seller templates.
4. Use **AI to generate pin titles and descriptions** with keywords:

   > "Generate 10 Pinterest pin titles and 10 SEO-rich descriptions for this product: [paste product info]. Include keywords teachers actually search. Each title under 100 characters, each description 200–500 characters."

5. Schedule pins with **Tailwind** ($15/mo) or post manually 2–3 times a day.

Pinterest is slow to start (3–6 months) but compounds. Pins from 2 years ago still drive traffic.

### Instagram and TikTok

- Optional. Useful if you enjoy short video. Skip if you don't.
- Best format: 15–30 second clips showing your resource in use—flipping through pages, students working on it, before/after.
- AI helps with **scripts, captions, and hashtag research**, not the videos themselves.

### SEO basics for your product titles

This single trick massively boosts visibility on TPT and Etsy:

**Bad title:** "Constitution Worksheet"
**Good title:** "Constitution & Bill of Rights Stations | 7th Grade US History | Print & Digital"

**More history examples:**

- "Ancient Egypt Unit | 6th Grade World History | Notes, Stations, Project | Print & Digital"
- "Industrial Revolution DBQ | 8th Grade US History | Primary Source Analysis"
- "Mesopotamia Escape Room | Digital & Print | 6th Grade Social Studies"
- "Civil War One-Pager Project | Middle School US History | Editable Template"

Front-load the grade and the specific topic. Add format details. Use the pipe character `|` to separate keyword phrases.

**AI prompt:**

> "I'm listing this product on TPT: [description]. Generate 5 SEO-optimized title options under 80 characters. Include grade level, specific topic, and format keywords (print, digital, no-prep, etc.)."

### A weekly rhythm you can actually sustain

This is the schedule that works for most teacher-sellers (~5 hours/week):

| Day | Task | Time |
|-----|------|------|
| Sunday | Plan the week, write next product description | 1 hr |
| Monday | Upload 1 new product or update an old one | 1 hr |
| Wednesday | Create and schedule 10 Pinterest pins | 1 hr |
| Friday | Send weekly email to list | 30 min |
| Saturday | Review stats, plan next product | 30 min |
| Anytime | Reply to buyer questions and reviews | 30 min |

## 6. An AI Toolkit for Daily Teaching Work

Even if the side-business never takes off, AI will save you hours every week as a working teacher. This section is the "free upgrade."

### Tools to know

| Tool | What it does | Cost | When to use |
|------|--------------|------|-------------|
| **ChatGPT** | General writing, planning, ideas | Free; $20/mo Plus | Everyday |
| **Claude** | Same; better for long documents | Free; $20/mo Pro | When you need to paste a whole unit or primary source |
| **Magicschool.ai** | Teacher-specific AI tools (IEPs, lesson plans, rubrics) | Free tier; $9.99/mo | Lesson prep |
| **Diffit** | Differentiated reading passages from any topic or URL | Free tier; $14/mo | **Huge for history** — paste a primary source, get leveled versions |
| **Canva** | Design (with AI image generation, magic write) | Free; $15/mo Pro | Product covers, classroom posters, slides |
| **NotebookLM** (Google) | Summarize PDFs, generate study guides from your own materials | Free | Drop in a whole chapter or set of primary sources, ask questions, generate review guides |
| **Library of Congress, National Archives, Avalon Project** | Free primary sources | Free | Source material you can actually trust |

### Prompt templates to steal

**Lesson plan from a standard or topic:**
> "Create a 45-minute lesson plan for 7th grade US history on [topic, e.g., 'causes of the American Revolution']. Include: hook (5 min, ideally a primary source or image), direct instruction (15 min), guided practice with a document analysis (15 min), independent practice (10 min). Add an exit ticket and 3 differentiation strategies (below/on/above grade level). Cite any primary sources used."

**Primary source scaffold:**
> "Take this primary source and make it accessible for 7th graders reading at a 6th grade level. Provide: (1) a 3-sentence context paragraph, (2) the source with hard vocabulary defined in brackets, (3) 4 scaffolded analysis questions moving from comprehension to inference to evaluation. Source: [paste]"

**Engaging hook ideas:**
> "Give me 5 hook ideas for a middle school lesson on [topic]. Each should take under 5 minutes and use one of: a primary source image, a 'would you rather,' a quick stat that surprises students, a 60-second story, or a controversial question. Be specific."

**Stations activity:**
> "Design a 6-station gallery walk for 8th grade US history on [topic]. For each station, give me: a title, the source/material used, a guiding question, an output (sketch, sentence, claim, etc.), and a difficulty level. Stations should rotate every 7 minutes."

**Mock trial / simulation:**
> "Design a 2-day mock trial / role-play simulation for 8th graders on [historical event]. Include: character roles with brief backstories, a scenario setup, evidence each side will use, deliberation guidelines, and a debrief discussion guide."

**Parent email (tricky topic):**
> "Help me write a professional, warm email to a parent about [situation, e.g., student missing assignments, behavior concern, positive update]. Tone: caring but professional. Suggest a next step like a meeting or phone call. Keep it under 200 words."

**IEP/504 language:**
> "Rewrite this accommodation in clearer, parent-friendly language while keeping it specific and measurable: [paste]."

**Rubric:**
> "Create a 4-point rubric for [assignment]. Criteria: [list 3–4]. For each level, write specific descriptors a student would understand."

**Differentiation on the fly:**
> "Take this worksheet and create three versions: (1) for students reading 2 years below grade level, (2) standard, (3) extension/enrichment. Keep the same skill focus."

**Sub plans:**
> "Generate a full day of sub plans for [grade]. Subjects: [list]. Include: morning routine, transitions, exact directions a substitute can read aloud, backup activities, and notes about students with specific needs (leave blanks for me to fill in)."

### Two rules

1. **Never paste student names or identifying info into an AI tool.** Use "Student A," "the student," etc. Treat it like you'd treat a public forum.
2. **Verify every historical fact, date, quote, and citation.** AI is fluent and confident — and frequently wrong about history. It invents quotes, misattributes sources, and confuses similarly-named events. Cross-check against a real source before it lands in a paid product.

## 7. First 30 Days: A Concrete Action Plan

### Week 1: Set up shop

- [ ] Open a free TPT seller account
- [ ] Pick 3 of your strongest existing resources
- [ ] Polish each one with the AI prompts in Section 2
- [ ] Design covers in Canva (use TPT cover templates)
- [ ] Write descriptions and SEO-optimized titles
- [ ] Upload all 3 products
- [ ] **Milestone:** Your store is live

### Week 2: Build your audience foundation

- [ ] Set up MailerLite or Kit (free)
- [ ] Create one really good free resource as a lead magnet
- [ ] Buy a domain and set up a basic Payhip store
- [ ] Add an email signup form linked to the freebie
- [ ] Write your "About" page with AI's help
- [ ] **Milestone:** You can collect email addresses

### Week 3: Start marketing

- [ ] Set up Pinterest business account
- [ ] Make 5 pin designs per product (15 pins total) in Canva
- [ ] Generate Pinterest titles and descriptions with AI
- [ ] Post pins manually, 2–3 per day for 7 days
- [ ] Send your first email to anyone who signed up
- [ ] **Milestone:** Traffic is starting

### Week 4: Review and iterate

- [ ] Check TPT analytics — what got the most views? Any sales?
- [ ] Check Pinterest analytics — which pin styles got saves?
- [ ] Add 2–3 more products based on what's getting attention
- [ ] Bundle your 3 starter products at a discount
- [ ] Plan next month: pick 1 thing to double down on
- [ ] **Milestone:** You have data, not guesses

### After Day 30

The pattern repeats: add products, refine marketing, grow your list. By month 3, aim for **10–15 products** and **100+ email subscribers**. That's the threshold where consistent sales usually begin.

## 8. Level Up: AI Agents on Your Computer

> **Skip this section for now.** It's here for later, when you've outgrown chat windows. The core business in Sections 1–7 doesn't need any of this.

Everything in this guide so far uses **AI chat in a web browser** (ChatGPT, Claude). That's perfect for one-off questions: paste a worksheet, get feedback, copy the result back. It's the right tool when you start.

But after 6–12 months you may hit a wall. Your workflow looks like this:

- "I have 47 worksheets in a folder. I need an answer key for each one."
- "I have 60 products. I want to rewrite every description to match my new branding."
- "I want to generate 200 Pinterest pin titles from my catalog spreadsheet."

Doing that in a chat window means copying-pasting 47 times. That's where **AI agents that run on your computer** become a different category of tool.

### What's an "agent"?

A chat AI gives you words to copy. **An agent reads your files, makes the changes, and saves them — all from one instruction.** It can:

- Open a folder of Word documents and edit each one
- Read a CSV and write a new column with AI-generated content
- Generate dozens of PDFs from a template
- Maintain a whole website by editing the underlying files

It's the same AI brain (Claude, GPT), just with hands and feet — access to your files and the ability to act.

### The current options (mid-2026)

| Tool | What it is | Cost | Best for |
|------|-----------|------|----------|
| **Claude Code** | Anthropic's terminal agent | $20/mo (in Claude Pro) or pay-as-you-go API | Heavy file work, technical projects |
| **GitHub Copilot CLI** | GitHub's terminal agent | $10/mo (Copilot Pro) | Same; cheaper if you already have Copilot |
| **Cursor** | An AI-first code editor (looks like a normal text editor) | $20/mo | Less intimidating UI than a terminal |
| **ChatGPT Desktop app** with file access | Lighter-weight; less powerful but no learning curve | Included with ChatGPT Plus | Light file work without a terminal |

If terminals scare you, **start with Cursor** — it feels like a Word-style editor with an AI sidebar.

### Concrete things to ask an agent to do

Once you have one set up, here are realistic tasks for a teacher-seller:

**File batch operations:**
> "Look at every .docx file in the /worksheets folder. For each one, generate a matching answer key and save it as `[original-name]-answer-key.pdf` in the /answer-keys folder."

**Catalog updates:**
> "Here's a CSV of my 60 TPT products with current descriptions. Rewrite each description using this new template [paste template], keep the same tone, save the result as a new CSV."

**Pin generation:**
> "Read products.csv. For each row, generate 10 Pinterest pin titles and 10 descriptions following [these rules]. Output as pins.csv with columns: product_name, title, description."

**Website maintenance (if he ever has one):**
> "Add a new product page for my Constitution Stations resource. Use the same layout as the Mesopotamia page. Pull product info from this paragraph: [paste]."

**Image processing:**
> "Take every image in /raw-photos, resize to 2000×1500, add my watermark, save to /processed."

### When it's worth the setup pain

You should consider agents when **any one of these is true**:

- You have more than ~30 products and any batch task takes you a full afternoon
- You maintain your own website (not just Payhip) and editing it feels like a chore
- You find yourself doing the same prompt over and over with different inputs
- You want to generate visual assets (pins, thumbnails, social posts) in bulk

If none of those are true yet, **stay with chat.** The setup cost isn't worth it.

### Getting started without getting stuck

The honest truth: the first hour with a CLI agent is rough if you've never used a terminal. Three options:

1. **Use Cursor instead of a terminal-based agent.** It hides the scary parts.
2. **Ask a friend (especially one who codes) to do the initial setup with you.** One hour together saves a week of frustration. *That's how you got this guide.*
3. **Wait 6 months.** This space is moving fast — by then there will likely be much friendlier tools aimed at non-developers.

### Privacy reminder, again

Same rule as Section 6: **never let an agent process student work, names, grades, or anything FERPA-protected.** Agents read whatever you point them at, and many send that content to a cloud AI. Keep your work files and your school files in separate folders, and only point agents at the work folder.

## 9. Appendix

### Glossary

- **TPT** — Teachers Pay Teachers
- **Lead magnet** — a free thing you give away in exchange for an email address
- **SEO** — Search Engine Optimization; getting your stuff to show up in searches
- **Bundle** — multiple products sold together at a discount
- **Lead time** — how long marketing takes to produce sales (Pinterest: 3–6 months; email: instant)
- **No-prep** — a resource a teacher can use without preparation; high-value keyword
- **Print & digital** — comes as both PDF and Google Slides/Forms; another high-value keyword

### Tools at a glance

| Need | Tool | Cost |
|------|------|------|
| AI writing | ChatGPT or Claude | Free; $20/mo for paid |
| Design | Canva | Free; $15/mo Pro |
| Marketplace | Teachers Pay Teachers | Free or $59.95/yr |
| Own store | Payhip | Free (5% fee) |
| Email | MailerLite | Free to 1,000 subs |
| Domain | Cloudflare / Namecheap | ~$10/yr |
| Pinterest scheduling | Tailwind | $15/mo |
| Business email | Google Workspace | $6/mo |

**Minimum monthly cost to start:** $0
**Realistic monthly cost once serious:** $40–$80

### Common pitfalls

1. **Using copyrighted clipart, fonts, or images.** TPT will pull your products. Use TPT-licensed clipart from sellers like Kate Hadfield, or generate your own with Canva/AI image tools. **For history images specifically:** the Library of Congress, National Archives, Wikimedia Commons (public domain category), and Met Museum Open Access are gold mines of legitimately free historical images.
2. **AI-generated content policies.** TPT requires you to disclose if a product is AI-generated. **Always have human review** — AI hallucinates dates, invents quotes, and fabricates "primary sources" that don't exist. For a history seller, your reputation depends on accuracy. Use AI to draft; you fact-check and finish.
3. **Pricing too low.** New sellers price at $1–$2 to "be competitive." Don't. Price like established sellers: $3–$5 for a single resource, $7–$15 for a bundle, $20–$50 for a unit.
4. **Spreading too thin.** Don't open 5 stores on 5 platforms in month one. Master TPT first.
5. **Treating it like a hobby for too long.** Set actual goals. Track actual numbers. Review monthly.
6. **Not backing up your files.** Keep originals in Google Drive or Dropbox, not just on your laptop.
7. **Forgetting taxes.** Save 25–30% of your earnings for taxes from day one. A side business is income.

### Quick prompt library (copy-paste)

**Polish a worksheet:**
> Review this teaching resource for a [grade] [subject] class. Check for: clear instructions, grade-appropriate vocabulary, alignment to [standard], missing answer key, opportunities to differentiate. Suggest specific changes. [paste]

**Write a product description:**
> Write a Teachers Pay Teachers product description for this resource. Format: 2-sentence hook, "What's Included" bullet list, "Perfect For" bullet list, "Standards" line, closing line. Voice: warm, professional, teacher-to-teacher. [paste]

**Generate cover ideas:**
> Suggest 5 cover designs for a TPT product called "[title]" aimed at [grade] teachers. For each: describe layout, color palette, fonts, and any visual elements. Keep designs clean and readable as a Pinterest thumbnail.

**Pinterest pin batch:**
> Generate 10 Pinterest pin titles and matching descriptions for this product: [paste]. Each title under 100 characters. Each description 200–500 characters with naturally placed keywords. Vary the angles (problem/solution, list, question, transformation).

**Weekly email:**
> Write a 200-word email to a list of [grade] teachers. Topic: [topic]. Include one teaching tip they can use Monday, then mention [product] as a related resource. Voice: friendly colleague. End with a question.

**Bundle pitch:**
> Write a description for a bundle containing these products: [list]. Highlight savings, completeness, and time saved. Include a "Bundle and save" headline.
